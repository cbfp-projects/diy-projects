# Side table next to the chair

**P-189** · someday. Not a buy.

Kate and Laurel **Aarstad** (**$190**, 16 × 14 × 22 in) and **Cakora** (**$230**, 22 × 13 × 22 in) are too expensive. Same job: a wood top with a lower shelf beside the **IKEA OSKARSHAMN** (seat about **17⅜ in**). The bought tables sit at **22 in**, about **4½ in** above the seat, at the front corner of the wing on the side used for feeding. The top holds a bottle and a phone. The shelf holds wipes.

## Design

Finished size **16 in wide × 14 in deep × 22 in tall**. Four **1×4** boards side by side make the 14 in depth with no ripping. Legs are **2×2**. Every joint is a 90° cut, glue, and a Kreg pocket screw. No miters.

```
16" top — four 1×4s, 14" deep
┌────────────────────┐
│                    │
└────────────────────┘
│                    │  legs 21¼" under the ¾" top
│                    │
├──── shelf slats ───┤  shelf surface 8" off the floor
│                    │
```

The 16 in side goes along the chair. The 14 in side points into the room. Same footprint as the Aarstad.

Actual sizes, which the cuts use:

| Nominal | Actual |
|---------|--------|
| 1×4 | ¾ × 3½ in |
| 2×2 | 1½ × 1½ in |

## Buy at Home Depot

Select pine, the straight ones. Sight down each board in the aisle and skip any bow or twist. A twisted 2×2 will rock.

- 3× [1×4 × 8 ft select pine](https://www.homedepot.com/p/1-in-x-4-in-x-8-ft-Select-Pine-Board-922484/202535808). Two boards cover the cut list. The third is for a knot or a bad end.
- 2× [2×2 × 8 ft select pine](https://www.homedepot.com/p/2-in-x-2-in-x-8-ft-Select-Pine-Board-532352/205031916). Four legs fit on one board if it is straight.
- Wood glue (Titebond II, or the store bottle).
- Kreg **1¼ in coarse-thread** screws, if the kit box is not already full. Coarse thread is for pine.
- 120 and 220 sandpaper.
- [Varathane clear satin water-based polyurethane, 1 qt](https://www.homedepot.com/p/Varathane-1-qt-Clear-Satin-Water-Based-Interior-Polyurethane-200241H/100169146). This table stays inside. Do not use the outdoor WaterGuard from the pine-table list.

Set the Kreg jig to **¾ in** for every hole in this plan.

## Cut list

Crosscut only, saw at 90°. A stop block on the fence makes the matching parts the same length. Check the first offcut with a square before cutting the set.

| Part | Qty | Size | From |
|------|-----|------|------|
| Legs | 4 | 2×2 × **21¼ in** | 2×2 |
| Top boards | 4 | 1×4 × **16 in** | 1×4 |
| Front and back rails | 2 | 1×4 × **13 in** | 1×4 |
| Side rails | 2 | 1×4 × **11 in** | 1×4 |
| Shelf stretchers | 2 | 1×4 × **11 in** | 1×4 |
| Shelf slats | 3 | 1×4 × **13 in** | 1×4 |

Rails are shorter than the top because they sit **between** the legs: 16 − 1½ − 1½ = 13, and 14 − 1½ − 1½ = 11.

Label each piece in pencil on a face that will be hidden.

## Pocket holes

Drill these before assembly. Holes face the inside, or the underside, so they disappear.

**Side rails and shelf stretchers** (the 11 in pieces). Two holes in each end. On the side rails, center the holes **¾ in** and **2¼ in** down from the top edge. On the stretchers, center them in the board. This offset keeps the screws out of the way of the front-rail screws inside the leg.

**Front and back rails** (the 13 in pieces). Two holes in each end, centered **1½ in** and **3 in** down from the top edge.

**Top boards.** Three holes along one long edge of three of the boards, from the underside, about 2 in in from each end and one in the middle. The fourth board has no edge holes. Also two holes along each end of every top board, from the underside, for screwing the top down onto the rails later. Keep those end holes 2 in in from the corners so they land on the rail, not in mid-air.

**Shelf slats.** Two holes at each end, from the underside.

Drive one test screw through two scrap 1×4s, and one into a scrap 2×2, before the real pieces. The tip should not come out the far side.

## Assembly

1. **Two end frames.** Each end is two legs, one 11 in side rail flush with the top of the legs, and one 11 in stretcher whose **top edge is 7¼ in** up from the bottom of the legs. Glue the rail and stretcher ends. Clamp. Drive the pocket screws. The two frames must match. Measure both stretchers from the **bottom** of the leg, not from the top.

2. **Connect the ends.** Stand the frames up. Glue and screw the 13 in front rail and the 13 in back rail between them, flush with the tops of the legs. Measure the two diagonals across the top opening. They should match. If they do not, rack the frame before the glue grabs.

3. **Shelf.** Lay the three 13 in slats across the stretchers. A ¼ in gap between them is right: 3 × 3½ in = 10½ in, and the opening is 11 in. Screw up through the pocket holes into the stretchers. No glue, so a slat can be replaced.

4. **Top panel.** Glue the long edges of the four 16 in boards. Clamp the seam closed, then drive the edge pocket screws from underneath. Keep the boards flat on the bench while you do this. Leave the clamps on for the glue time on the bottle. The panel should come out 16 × 14.

5. **Attach the top.** When the panel is dry, set it on the frame, flush with the outside of the legs. Drive the end pocket screws up into the underside of the top. Do not glue the top to the rails.

6. **Sand.** 120, then 220. Knock down the sharp edges. Wipe with a damp cloth, let it dry, and sand once more with 220 so the poly does not raise the grain.

7. **Finish.** Three coats of the water-based poly on the top, the shelf, and any face a hand will touch. Two hours between coats. It is low odor, which matters next to the chair.

## Before you call it done

Set it next to the OSKARSHAMN. The top should land about **4½ in** above the seat. If the table rocks, sand the bottom of the long leg rather than adding a shim.
